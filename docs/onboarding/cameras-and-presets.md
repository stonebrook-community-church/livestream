# Cameras and Presets

This page covers where the two cameras are, what each saved shot looks like, how you recall shots with the SuperJoy, and why you only move the camera that isn't live.

A **preset** is a saved camera position. You recall it with one button on the SuperJoy. For other video terms, see [Video Basics](video-basics.md).

## Where the cameras are

Cam 2 is on the left side and sees the stage at an angle; its P5 view turns toward the congregation.](../assets/diagrams/room-map.svg)
  <figcaption>Top-down map, not to scale. Cam 1 is at the back center. Cam 2 is on the left side when you face the stage. The shaded areas show what each camera sees on P1.</figcaption>

- **Cam 1 — center.** It looks straight at the stage from the back of the room. It gives the "front row" view, so it is the main camera for the worship leader, announcements and the sermon.
- **Cam 2 — left side**, when you face the stage. It sees the stage at an angle. That makes it good for the band, the communion table and the side view of the preacher in Scene 7 — Sermon Split.

Both are PTZ cameras: a motor pans, tilts and zooms them. You control them from the booth.

## How the preset numbers work

- **P1 is always the safe wide shot**, on both cameras. If you are unsure, P1 is never wrong.
- **The same number means the same kind of shot** on both cameras where possible. P1 is wide on both. P2 is the music shot on both. P3 is the pulpit on both.
- **Cam 1 has P1 to P4. Cam 2 has P1 to P5.** Cam 2 P5 is only ever shown blurred, in Scene 8 — Break.

The presets are saved by the Livestream Lead. You only recall them. If a preset looks wrong, use another shot and tell the Livestream Lead after the service.

## Using the SuperJoy

The **SuperJoy** is the joystick controller for both cameras. Each preset button is labeled with the same name this site uses, for example `Cam 1 P3`.

To recall a preset:

1. Check which camera is live. Look at the [table on Video Basics](video-basics.md#program-and-preview) if you aren't sure.
2. On the SuperJoy, select the **other** camera, the one that is off air.
3. Press the preset number.
4. Wait until the camera stops moving.
5. Press the scene button on the Stream Deck to cut to that camera.

<div class="photo-placeholder">PHOTO NEEDED: superjoy.jpg</div>

!!! danger "Only move the camera that isn't live"
    Recall the preset on the off-air camera, wait for it to settle, then cut. If you move the live camera, every viewer sees the picture swing and zoom. Why this matters: [Operating Rules](rules.md#1-only-move-the-camera-that-isnt-live).

!!! tip "Use presets, not the joystick"
    During the service, recall presets instead of steering with the joystick. Presets look the same every week. If a shot isn't working, for example the speaker stepped out of frame, cut to the other camera or to a P1 wide shot.

!!! tip "Think one move ahead"
    While one camera is live, get the other camera ready for the next part of the service. The [Sunday Flow](sunday-flow.md) page shows what to prepare at each step.

## Exposure

**Exposure** is how bright the picture is, plus its color. A camera sets it with four settings: **iris** (how much light the lens lets in), **shutter** (how long each frame collects light), **gain** (electronic brightening, which adds grain) and **white balance** (which color counts as white).

Both cameras use **fixed manual exposure**. The same values are saved into every preset on that camera.

Why fixed exposure:

- **The stage lighting is the same every week.** Automatic exposure would keep adjusting anyway, for example when someone in a white shirt walks into the shot. The picture would pulse brighter and darker.
- **The two cameras need to match.** If one camera looks warmer or darker than the other, every cut between them is obvious.
- **It works around a known camera difference.** One of our cameras stores exposure with each preset. The other keeps whatever exposure it currently has. Saving the same fixed values into every preset makes both behave the same way.

You don't change exposure. During the setup checklist, you check that brightness and color look the same on every preset. If they don't, call the on-call tech lead before the service.
